Our ethos and aims

Ambitious, respectful, resilient and kind

These four values guide everything we do. Our aim is to deliver a broad, balanced and ambitious curriculum, provide the experiences and skills pupils need for further education, employment and active roles in society, and support pupils' needs so they can access, enjoy and succeed in school.

A welcome from our Headteacher

Meeting every young person exactly where they are

Welcome to Futures Matter - Northwood. It is a genuine privilege to introduce myself as the founding Headteacher of this exciting new provision, opening in the heart of Birmingham for secondary age young people whose primary need is SEMH and ASC.

At Northwood, we are building a school where every young person is met exactly where they are, and where we hold high challenge and high aspiration alongside a genuinely low threat, low barrier environment. We welcome visits, so that you can experience Northwood first hand. I look forward to meeting you and your child soon.

Benjamin Neasom
Founding Headteacher

Our pupils

Learning that fits the young person

Our pupils have a range of needs, including moderate learning difficulties, speech, language and communication needs, autism, and social, emotional and mental health needs. Pupil voice shapes how we teach, and pupils help plan their own learning.

Safe and known

A trauma-informed, relational approach

When a young person feels safe, understood and valued, they are ready to re-engage with learning, often for the first time in years.

Support

Therapeutic and clinical input

Speech and language therapy and other support are arranged as needs are identified, and we are training an in-house Thrive practitioner.

Voice

Pupils have a real say

The views of pupils shape curriculum planning, annual reviews and transition reviews, and we check in regularly about how school is working.

Community

Part of the neighbourhood

We help pupils become valued members of their local community through enrichment, work-related learning and life skills.

Curriculum and enrichment

An ambitious curriculum with plenty to enjoy

A broad curriculum based on the National Curriculum, shaped to each pupil's needs and interests. Every pupil reads for a protected 30 minutes a day.

Core

Maths, English and Science

Maths and English every day, using White Rose Maths, White Rose Science and Pearson English schemes of work.

Humanities

History, Geography and R.S.

Taught through shared themes, with a Theme of the Week to bring learning together.

Hands on

Food Technology

Our teaching kitchen is the classroom for cooking, nutrition and independence. Theory first, then practical.

Active

Physical Education

Regular PE at an offsite venue builds health, teamwork and confidence.

Digital

Computing and Digital

Practical digital skills and safe, confident use of technology.

Life skills

PSHE as a golden thread

Personal, social, health and economic education runs through every subject, building wellbeing and resilience.

Futures

Careers and work-related learning

Independent careers guidance and work experience prepare pupils for college, training and work.

Enrichment

Offsite Enrichment Fridays

Community and life-skills experiences that bring learning to life.

Qualifications and next steps

Recognised qualifications, personal pathways

Year 7 and 8

Accreditation starts in Year 7 with the AQA Unit Award Scheme and Entry Level qualifications.

By Year 9

Pupils can sit Level 1 Functional Skills.

Years 10 and 11

A personalised pathway of Functional Skills, Entry Level Certificates, GCSEs and equivalents, plus life skills and vocational awards such as Basic First Aid, Food Hygiene, The King's Trust awards and Duke of Edinburgh's Award.

Looking ahead

Learning for the real world

We are planning for pupils to take part in running a community cafe enterprise. It would build teamwork, customer service, food skills and confidence, and give pupils a real part to play in their community.

A Fresh Start

A fresh start for secondary‑age pupils.

Choosing the right school for any child is a life changing decision, and we know how much rides on getting it right. Futures Matter Northwood is a DfE‑registered, independent, specialist day school in Highgate, Birmingham, providing high quality education for boys and girls aged 11 to 16. Pupils have a range of needs, including moderate learning difficulties, speech, language and communication difficulties, autism, and social, emotional and mental health needs.

Futures Matter Education Ltd was set up by a group of highly experienced, like‑minded professionals, well known for supporting children and young people with a range of needs. Our company name reflects what we firmly believe: that everyone's future matters.

We know that sourcing suitable education can be a huge challenge for parents, carers and local authorities. Sometimes the right provision is full, or does not yet exist. Our solution was to set up a new service, so individuals can access relevant learning while being supported to attend or return to school.

We aim to:

  • Deliver a broad, balanced and ambitious curriculum, differentiated to individual needs and interests
  • Provide the experiences and skills pupils need for their next stage of life: further education, employment, and active roles in society
  • Support pupils' needs so they can access, enjoy and succeed in school
  • Ensure staff are well supported to deliver excellent education, through ongoing professional development

The Story of Our Building

Every building has a story. Ours is still being written.

For generations, 122 Emily Street has been part of our family, originally home to Northwood Engineering Company (B'ham) Limited, a family‑run British engineering business established in Birmingham in 1985. For nearly 40 years, this building was a place of industry, skill and endeavour: precision milling, drilling and turning, built on craftsmanship, accuracy and hard work. Those values have never left the building.

As the engineering chapter came to a close, our family was determined that the building should not simply be sold and its history forgotten. Where it once helped manufacture precision components, it is now a place for shaping something even more important: young people's futures. The machinery and workshops have given way to classrooms and learning spaces, but the underlying principles remain remarkably similar: patience, precision, perseverance, practical skills, pride in achievement, and the understanding that every individual has potential when given the right tools and support.

The purpose may have changed, but the ambition has not: to create, to develop, to take pride in what we do, and to build something that lasts.
Rick AsthanaCo‑Founder

Our Vision, Ethos & Values

Developing pupils as individuals, in an environment that is both enjoyable and safe.

Our aim is for learners to fully invest in the learning experience, because they see what's in it for them, and to embrace opportunities that help them succeed in life beyond school. At all Key Stages, our curriculum is structured to help learners secure success academically, personally, developmentally and in life beyond school.

Ambitious

Respectful

Kind

Our leaders are determined to provide a positive and welcoming atmosphere and culture. Our staff team are proud and committed to supporting pupils to secure successful futures. Our core principles also mean that we:

  • Meet pupils' needs and build on their strengths
  • Harness creativity, skills and interests
  • Identify support to manage and overcome barriers and difficulties
  • Develop self‑confidence, esteem and resilience
  • Support pupils to become valued members of our local community

Pupil Voice

We believe pupils should have a genuine say in their own education. Pupils' views are sought and valued as part of curriculum planning, annual reviews and transition reviews, and we regularly check in with pupils about how school is working for them. Listening to what pupils tell us, about their interests, their worries and what helps them learn, shapes how we support each individual and how Futures Matter Northwood continues to develop as a school.

Our Curriculum

Ambition and emotional safety depend on each other.

We believe every pupil has a personal best, and our trauma‑informed approach is designed to help them achieve it. Our curriculum is pupil‑centred, ambitious, relevant and engaging: high‑quality schemes of work, including White Rose Maths, White Rose Science and Pearson English, are tailored to each pupil's needs, interests and learning profile.

Nurture Pathway

For pupils who need to build confidence, regulation and engagement first. Personalised, skills‑based learning in subjects that motivate and interest them.

Academic Pathway

A broad, ambitious academic curriculum leading to GCSEs and equivalents, for pupils ready for more traditional subject‑based study.

High challenge, low barrier

We hold high aspirations within a genuinely low‑threat, low‑barrier environment, recognising that pupils with SEMH and ASC needs can only access an ambitious curriculum once they feel safe, regulated and understood. Core subjects are taught through this approach, with humanities (history, geography and religious studies) taught as separate subjects on a rotating basis, enhanced by our ‘Themes of the Week’.

Hands‑on and everyday learning

Food Technology and Physical Education provide regular hands‑on, practical learning opportunities. Personal, Social, Health and Economic (PSHE) education runs as a golden thread throughout all subjects and school activities, and every pupil reads for a protected 30 minutes each day.

Accreditation

Accreditation starts in Year 7 with the AQA Unit Award Scheme and Entry Level qualifications; by Year 9, pupils can sit Level 1 Functional Skills. At Key Stage 4, pupils follow a personalised pathway of accreditation options, which may include Functional Skills, Entry Level Certificates, GCSEs and equivalents, alongside life skills and vocational qualifications such as Basic First Aid, Food Hygiene, The King's Trust awards, and Duke of Edinburgh's Award.

Healthy lifestyles

We support pupils to understand the benefits of living a healthy lifestyle and making good life choices, starting from a healthy breakfast on arrival.

Each pupil's outcomes are carefully tailored to their Education, Health and Care Plan (EHCP) and personal aspirations. We work closely with pupils and families to set ambitious yet achievable goals, reviewing progress regularly so that every child reaches their personal best. As our cohort of pupils grows, we will continue to adapt our curriculum, which is kept under at least annual review by our Head Teacher, Proprietor and Chair of School Governance Board.

Access to examinations and results

Pupils who are academically and emotionally ready to complete tests and examinations will do so at the appropriate time. Because only a small number of pupils sit public examinations each year, results are not published on our website, to avoid identifying any individuals concerned. Results are reported to parents and carers by personal letter on the relevant results day, and every parent and carer receives an annual report of their child's progress and attainment.

Relationships, Sex and Religious Education

All pupils receive age and ability appropriate Relationships and Sex Education (RSE), vital in preparing them to recognise and sustain healthy, safe relationships. Our Religious Studies offer, delivered as part of our humanities rotation, helps pupils understand the core values of the main religions, referring to Birmingham's Locally Agreed RE Syllabus, promoting tolerance and respect for diversity.

Personal & Social Development

Developing the whole child, alongside academic progress.

We take a trauma‑informed, relational approach to developing the whole child. When a pupil does not yet feel safe, regulated and connected, this affects their readiness to learn, so building trust, co‑regulation and emotional wellbeing, informed by Thrive principles, sits alongside academic progress at every stage.

Trauma‑Informed Practice

Staff are trained in trauma informed, relational practice, putting pupils' emotional needs first so they can re‑engage with learning.

Relationships & Sex Education

Careers & Work‑Related Learning

Behaviour, Rewards & Sanctions

Pupils are recognised and rewarded for positive engagement and progress, with personalised targets linked to their EHCP.

Our staff complete comprehensive GCM (Global Conflict Management) Therapeutic De‑escalation and Physical Intervention training, an accredited programme grounded in trauma‑informed principles and the belief that physical intervention is always a last resort. This supports staff to promote positive behaviours, use therapeutic de‑escalation techniques, and keep everyone safe. Pupils are recognised and awarded points for positive engagement, with personalised targets based on their EHCP objectives.

Class sizes and staffing

We keep classes small so that every pupil is known well and supported closely. Classes are capped at 5 pupils, with a Teacher/Instructor and a Teaching Assistant in every class. This allows staff to adapt pace, pitch and approach to each pupil's needs moment by moment, rather than pupils having to fit a one‑size‑fits‑all lesson.

Therapeutic and clinical support

Alongside our trauma‑informed, relational approach to teaching, pupils have access to Speech and Language Therapy and other clinical input, arranged through service level agreements as individual pupils' needs are identified. We are also training an in‑house Thrive Practitioner to further strengthen our emotional and social development offer on site. Support is matched to what's set out in a pupil's EHCP, and we work closely with parents, carers and other professionals to ensure this support is joined up.

First aid, health and medical needs

Our school has a medical room and safe storage of medication. Staff complete Basic First Aid Training, with a three yearly refresher. Individualised Risk Assessments are agreed with parents and carers for pupils with specific health needs. Nominated staff are trained to administer prescribed medication, which is recorded and kept in a locked medical cabinet.

Health and safety, risk management

Health and Safety is coordinated by the Head Teacher. All staff complete Health and Safety awareness training, and maintenance contractors complete comprehensive site checks, with regular audits. Risk assessments across all curriculum activities and the environment are reviewed, authorised and signed off by the Head Teacher before activities take place, including a full Fire Risk Assessment reviewed annually with fire services.

Anti‑bullying

Everyone in our school community is entitled to feel valued, safe and protected from harm, including from bullying, in line with NSPCC best practice guidance. We are a ‘telling school’, where pupils, parents, carers and staff are encouraged to raise concerns, and every report is listened to, taken seriously and responded to promptly. We work with both those who are bullied and those who bully to understand what has happened and to repair harm, and our curriculum teaches and promotes British Values of tolerance, respect, equality and diversity.

Safeguarding

Keeping every child safe.

Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility at Futures Matter Northwood. Many of our pupils have had experiences that make life and school difficult, so keeping them safe, and helping them feel safe, comes before everything else we do.

We follow Keeping Children Safe in Education (KCSIE) and the procedures of the Birmingham Safeguarding Children Partnership. We work closely with families, Birmingham Children's Trust, the police, health services and other agencies whenever a child needs extra help or protection.

All staff receive safeguarding training when they join and at least once every year, and everyone who works with our pupils is checked under our safer recruitment procedures. If you are worried about any child, however small the worry, please tell us. We will always listen, take it seriously and act sensitively.

What does safeguarding mean?

Safeguarding means protecting children from abuse and neglect, looking after their health and development, making sure they grow up in safe and caring circumstances, and helping every child to do as well as they can. It covers child protection, bullying, online safety, health and safety, first aid, keeping children safe from extremism, and making sure the adults who work with them are suitable to do so.

Attendance

We know that many of our pupils have experienced difficulties with attendance in previous settings, and we work closely with pupils, parents and carers to understand and address any barriers. Regular attendance gives pupils the best chance to access the therapeutic and academic support on offer, and parents/carers are kept informed of term dates, inset days and holidays well in advance.

Supporting Your Child's EHCP

Regular reviews, with every voice heard.

Annual Review meetings

An early Annual Review meeting is called within the first 12 weeks of admission, giving pupil, parents/carers, staff and local authority the opportunity to discuss integration, assessments, support programmes, and EHCP objectives, and to agree the date of the next full Annual Review.

Transition Review meetings

Held at key transition points (for example, Years 6, 9 and 11) with the views of the pupil, parents/carers, staff, clinicians and Local Authorities heard and minuted. These meetings focus on whether EHCP objectives have been met and/or need updating, and pupils have a Transition Plan to support a successful move between phases. A proforma is sent out ahead of these meetings, and we encourage families to take time to consider their responses in advance.

Our Admissions Process

How a placement with us begins.

We specialise in supporting children aged 11 to 16 with moderate learning difficulties, communication and interaction needs, autism, or social, emotional and mental health needs. Referrals must come to us directly from the pupil's local authority, at any point in the year.

Referral

Referrals come directly from the pupil's local authority, and are considered by our Admissions Panel.

Meeting

Where we believe we might be able to meet a child's needs, we arrange to meet the child and their parent or carer to learn more, before a place is offered.

Visit & taster

We offer a home or school visit and taster session, so pupils and families can experience our provision first hand.

Decision

We must be certain that we can meet a child's needs effectively and that the placement is likely to be successful for everyone concerned. We confirm this, and a contract, fees and transport arrangements, if applicable, are agreed with the local authority.

Placement

A start date is agreed: always the earliest date we can facilitate a smooth, personalised admission. All pupils have a personalised Admission Programme supporting a smooth transition.

Please refer to our Admissions Policy, available on request from the school office. If you wish to discuss a referral, please contact our Headteacher, Benjamin Neasom, through the school office at office@futuresmattereducation.co.uk.

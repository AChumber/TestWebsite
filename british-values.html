British values

Preparing pupils for life in modern Britain

Our pupils are growing up in a diverse, fast-changing society. We help every young person understand the values that bring us together, and the part they can play in their community.

Our statement

How we promote fundamental British values

The Department for Education expects schools to promote the fundamental British values of democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs. At Futures Matter - Northwood we do this through our curriculum, our trauma-informed, relational approach, and the way adults and pupils treat each other every day. Our pupils have a range of needs, so we teach these values in ways that make sense to each young person, and we recognise the different cultural backgrounds of our pupils and staff.

1

Democracy

Every voice matters, and decisions are better when people are heard.

  • Pupils' views are sought and valued in curriculum planning, annual reviews and transition reviews.
  • We regularly check in with pupils about how school is working for them, and what we hear shapes how we support each individual.
  • Through PSHE and Humanities, pupils learn how decisions are made in our society and why it matters to listen to others.
2

The rule of law

Rules and laws are there to keep everyone safe and to make things fair.

  • Our expectations are clear and consistent, and are set out in our Behaviour, Rewards and Sanctions Policy.
  • When something goes wrong, we help pupils understand what happened, take responsibility and repair harm.
  • Relationships and Sex Education, PSHE and our safeguarding work teach pupils about keeping safe and about the laws that protect them.
3

Individual liberty

Pupils are supported to make choices, and to understand the responsibility that comes with them.

  • Pupils help shape their own targets, and their views are central to their reviews.
  • Careers education, off-site enrichment and community-based life skills build independence and confidence.
  • We teach pupils how to use their freedoms safely and responsibly, including online and with their own Chromebook.
4

Mutual respect and tolerance of those of different faiths and beliefs

We respect each other, and we value difference.

  • Our curriculum teaches and promotes tolerance, respect, equality and diversity.
  • Religious Studies helps pupils understand the core values of the main religions, following Birmingham's Locally Agreed RE Syllabus.
  • Bullying is not tolerated. We work with those who are bullied and those who bully to understand what has happened and repair harm.

Beyond the four values

More about how we prepare pupils

Spiritual, moral, social and cultural development

Pupils' spiritual, moral, social and cultural (SMSC) development runs through our curriculum, our ethos and everyday relationships. We help pupils reflect on their own beliefs and experiences, tell right from wrong, work well with others, and appreciate the culture and heritage that have shaped modern Britain and the diverse city of Birmingham.

Part of our community

Our off-site enrichment programme and community projects help pupils play an active part in their local community, and prepare them to take responsibility as citizens. Our proposed community cafe will give pupils the chance to help run an enterprise project.

Rights Respecting Schools

We are working towards Rights Respecting Schools status with UNICEF UK. Teaching pupils about their rights, and the rights of others, supports individual liberty and mutual respect every day.

Our own school values

Our school values are being ambitious, respectful, resilient and kind. They sit alongside British values and guide how we treat each other every day. Read our ethos and aims.

SEND Local Offer and SEN information report

What we offer pupils with SEND

This page sets out how we identify, support and review the special educational needs of every pupil. It is based on our SEND Policy and is written for parents, carers, pupils and local authorities.

Who we are for

Futures Matter - Northwood is an independent special school for up to 20 pupils aged 11 to 16. Every pupil has an education, health and care (EHC) plan. Our pupils have a range of needs, including moderate learning difficulties, speech, language and communication needs, autism, and social, emotional and mental health needs.

Communication and interaction

Many pupils are on the autism spectrum or have speech, language and communication needs. We use clear routines, visual supports and adults who check understanding.

Cognition and learning

Pupils who learn at a slower pace, or who have dyslexia or similar needs, get adapted teaching, small steps and structured reading every day.

Social, emotional and mental health

This is the main need of our pupils. We understand behaviour as communication and respond with relationships, regulation and support before sanctions.

Sensory and physical

We provide calm and nurture spaces, and make reasonable adjustments to the environment for sensory and physical needs.

How we support each pupil

We use a four-part cycle for every pupil, repeated each time with a better understanding of what helps.

Assess

Teacher and Headteacher use the EHC plan, our own assessments and the views of the pupil and family.

Plan

We agree adjustments, support and expected outcomes with the pupil and their parents or carers.

Do

Staff deliver the plan consistently and record what they see.

Review

We review at least every term, and sooner if needed.

Starting at Northwood

Fresh Start conversation

Before a pupil starts we listen to the pupil and family: what has worked, what has not, what matters and what worries them.

A paced induction

Induction is paced to the pupil and may take a few days or several weeks. We get to know the pupil's strengths and needs and write an Individual Support Plan with the family.

Everyone is prepared

All staff who will work with the pupil know their needs and the agreed approach before they arrive.

Working together

Families

We meet each term to review provision and progress, keep records that we share, and send a written progress report at least once a year.

Pupils

Pupils say what their strengths and difficulties are, help set targets, attend review meetings and tell us what works.

EHC plans and annual reviews

We hold an annual review of every EHC plan and early reviews when needs change. From Year 9 reviews focus on preparing for adulthood.

Therapeutic and specialist support

Speech and language therapy and other clinical input are arranged through service level agreements as needs are identified. We are training an in-house Thrive practitioner. Support is matched to what is in each EHC plan.

Early and transition reviews

An early Annual Review is held within 12 weeks of admission. Transition reviews at key points (for example on entry to Year 7, and in Years 9 and 11) hear the views of the pupil, family, staff, clinicians and local authority.

English as an additional language

Support is identified during referral and put in place before admission, including a bilingual teaching assistant or an interpreter for meetings.

Preparing for adulthood and moving on

Independent careers guidance

All pupils receive independent careers guidance, with work experience planned around their aspirations.

Year 11 transition

We work with the pupil, family, local authority and post-16 providers on visits, taster days and a handover, so there is a place and a plan before they leave.

Accreditation

Pupils start with the AQA Unit Award Scheme and Entry Level qualifications. By Year 9 they can sit Level 1 Functional Skills. In Years 10 and 11 pathways may include Functional Skills, Entry Level Certificates, GCSEs, Basic First Aid, Food Hygiene, The King's Trust awards and Duke of Edinburgh's Award.

Exams and access arrangements

We assess the need for arrangements such as extra time, rest breaks, a reader or a separate room early, so SEND is not a barrier.

Staff training and outside agencies

Training

All staff are trained in the needs of our pupils, including SEMH, autism, trauma, sensory processing and communication, and in the GCM approach. Training happens at induction and regularly through the year.

Working with others

We work with the placing local authority, educational psychologists, speech and language and occupational therapists, CAMHS, health professionals, social workers and post-16 providers.

Concerns, complaints and independent advice

Raise a concern

Talk first to your child's class teacher or the Headteacher. If it is not resolved, you can make a formal complaint under our Complaints Policy (FME-GD-01).

Independent advice (SENDIASS)

Free, confidential advice. For pupils placed by Birmingham City Council: Birmingham SENDIASS, SENDIASS@Birmingham.gov.uk, 0121 303 5004, PO Box 16270, Birmingham B2 2HN.

EHC plan decisions

Decisions about plans are made by the local authority. Families can ask for disagreement resolution or mediation and can appeal to the First-tier Tribunal (SEND). We will help you find the right route.

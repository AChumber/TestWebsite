Admissions

How to apply to Northwood

We specialise in supporting children aged 11 to 16 with moderate learning difficulties, communication and interaction needs, autism, or social, emotional and mental health needs.

Our process

From referral to moving on

We must be certain that we can meet a child's needs effectively and that the placement is likely to be successful for everyone concerned.

Part one

Admission

  1. 1

    Referral

    Referrals come directly from the pupil's local authority, at any point in the year. Our Admissions Panel, made up of the Headteacher and the Proprietor, considers each one.

    ➜
  2. 2

    Meeting

    If we may be able to meet the child's needs, we meet the child and their parent or carer.

    ➜
  3. 3

    Visit and taster

    A home or school visit and a taster session let pupils and families experience our school first hand.

    ➜
  4. 4

    Decision

    We confirm the placement is likely to succeed for everyone, and agree a contract, fees and any transport.

    ➜
  5. 5

    Placement

    A start date is agreed, always the earliest date we can offer a smooth, personalised admission.

Part two

Settling in, reviews and moving on

  1. 1

    Admission Programme

    Every pupil has a personalised Admission Programme, paced to them. Our Learner Discovery Framework shapes it, so starting school is a smooth transition.

    ➜
  2. 2

    Early review

    An early Annual Review within the first 12 weeks looks at settling in, support and EHC plan objectives.

    ➜
  3. 3

    Annual reviews

    Pupil, parents or carers, staff and the local authority meet each year to check progress and plan ahead.

    ➜
  4. 4

    Transition reviews

    At key points, such as entry to Year 7 and in Years 9 and 11, we agree a Transition Plan with everyone involved.

    ➜
  5. 5

    Moving on

    Pupils are supported to a suitable post-16 place, with visits, taster days and a handover, so there is a plan before they leave.

Working together

Building an individual learning pathway, together

Through admission and transition, we work with each young person and their family, through co-production, to create an individual learning pathway. We use our Learner Discovery Framework to do this, over an initial six-week transition period that is paced to each pupil.

  1. 1

    Listen first

    We start with what the young person and their family tell us, alongside the information we receive from the local authority, previous schools and other professionals.

    ➜
  2. 2

    Get to know the learner

    We look at readiness to learn, barriers, strengths and prior learning, in context. Relationships and emotional regulation come first, using nurture based approaches such as the Boxall Profile.

    ➜
  3. 3

    Look at areas of development

    We think about executive function, physical and sensory needs, speech, language and communication, and social and emotional development, rather than relying on labels.

    ➜
  4. 4

    Draft the profile

    By the end of the transition period we have a draft digital Individual Support Profile, with the pupil's own voice at its centre.

    ➜
  5. 5

    Agree the pathway

    Pupil, family and staff agree the individual learning pathway together, then review it as the pupil grows.

High ambition for every pupil. What we discover about a young person is used to plan the right support. It is never used to limit what they can achieve.

Who can be admitted

  • Boys and girls aged 11 to 16
  • Pupils with an EHC plan that names the school, or whom a local authority is considering for placement
  • Pupils whose needs we can meet and who can be educated safely alongside others

Referrals must come from the pupil's local authority. Pupils without an EHC plan are not admitted.

After admission

  • Every pupil has a personalised Admission Programme for a smooth transition
  • An early Annual Review is held within the first 12 weeks, to discuss settling in, assessments, support and EHC plan objectives
  • Transition reviews are held at key points, such as on entry to Year 7, and in Years 9 and 11
  • If English is not the first language of a pupil or parent, we identify support before admission, including a bilingual teaching assistant or an interpreter for meetings

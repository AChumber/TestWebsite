Safeguarding

Keeping every child safe

Our first duty is the safety and wellbeing of every pupil. This page explains how we keep children safe in school and online, and where families can get help in Birmingham, Solihull and nationally.

Worried about a child?

Email our safeguarding inboxsafeguarding@futuresmattereducation.co.ukRead by our safeguarding team. Please do not use the website enquiry form to report a concern.
  • If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.
  • To speak to someone, call the school office on 0121 835 1360 and ask for a member of our safeguarding team.
  • Children and young people can call Childline free, any time, on 0800 1111. Adults can call the NSPCC helpline on 0808 800 5000.

Our approach

How we keep pupils safe

Following the latest guidance

We follow Keeping Children Safe in Education (KCSIE), the statutory safeguarding guidance for schools, in its latest version (KCSIE 2026, in force from 1 September 2026). Safeguarding is at the heart of all we do.

Our Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy

Our policy sets out how we protect pupils, how concerns are recorded and acted on, and who to tell. Read the policy (FME-SG-01).

Everyone has a part to play

All staff are trained to spot signs of harm and to pass on any concern straight away to the DSL. We listen to pupils, take what they say seriously and act on it.

Safer recruitment

We follow safer recruitment practice, including pre-employment checks, so that everyone who works with our pupils has been properly vetted.

Online safety

Safe in school and safe online

Our approach follows the latest KCSIE and DfE guidance on filtering and monitoring and on mobile phones.

Filtering and monitoring

KCSIE requires schools to have appropriate filtering and monitoring on school devices and networks, and to review it at least once every academic year, keeping a record. We do this to stop pupils reaching harmful content, and to spot concerns early.

A mobile phone free school

In line with DfE guidance, our school day is free from mobile phones. Any phone brought onto site is stored in a special pouch at reception. Read more on our Key information page and in our Mobile Phone Policy (FME-BE-05) in the Behaviour policies.

Online safety and acceptable use

Pupils are taught how to stay safe online, and every pupil agrees to use school devices, including their Chromebook, responsibly. Read our Online Safety and Acceptable Use Policy (FME-SG-02).

Preventing extremism

We protect pupils from the risks of radicalisation and extremism through our curriculum and our Prevent duty. Read our Prevent policy (FME-SG-04).

Reporting online harm

If something has happened to a child online, report it to the CEOP Safety Centre. Sharing nude or sexual images of under-18s is a crime, including images made with AI, and is always treated as a safeguarding concern.

Allegations about adults

If you are worried about how an adult who works with children is behaving, tell the Headteacher. If your concern is about the Headteacher, write to the Chair of the School Governance Board, c/o the school office.

Getting help locally

Local safeguarding contacts

Birmingham: Children's Advice and Support Service (CASS)

The single point of contact for concerns about a child who lives in Birmingham. Call 0121 303 1888 (Monday to Thursday 8.45am to 5.15pm, Friday 8.45am to 4.15pm). Out of hours, call 0121 675 4806. Worried about a child? Birmingham City Council.

Solihull: Children's Social Care (MASH)

The front door for concerns about a child who lives in Solihull. Call 0121 788 4300. Out of hours, call 0121 605 6060. Solihull Safeguarding Children Partnership.

Police

In an emergency call 999. To report a crime or concern that is not an emergency, call 101.

Operation Encompass

We take part in Operation Encompass, so police can tell the DSL when a child has been present at an incident of domestic abuse, and we can give them the right support the next day. Find out more.

If your child is placed by another local authority, please contact the school office and we will give you the right contacts.

For parents and carers

Keeping children safe online at home

Talk to your child

Ask what they enjoy online, who they talk to and how they feel about it. Make sure they know they can always tell you or us if something upsets them, and will not be in trouble.

Use parental controls

Set up controls on phones, tablets, consoles and your home broadband, and check age ratings on apps and games.

Know where to report

If something does not feel right, report it to the CEOP Safety Centre, or tell us. You do not need to be sure that a child is at risk to ask for advice.

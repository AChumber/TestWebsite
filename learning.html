Structure helps

Why our day is shaped this way

Pupils with SEMH and ASC needs learn best when the day is predictable and every adult is clear about what happens next. The same timetable runs for every tutor group, and it is displayed in each classroom as a visual schedule. Classes are capped at five pupils, with a teacher or instructor and a teaching assistant in every class. Where a pupil's EHCP or needs require it, their personal timetable may vary, for example with fewer transitions or a base room.

Maths and English come first

They are taught every day in Periods 1 and 2, when pupils are most alert, so regular practice builds fluency and confidence.

Welcome and breakfast before learning

Pupils who arrive hungry, anxious or unsettled are welcomed, fed and settled first. Breakfast and tutor time teach routine, social skills and healthy choices.

Reading is protected

Structured Reading takes place for 30 minutes every day, whatever else is happening.

Regulation is planned

A 10-minute check-in after lunch, and reflection and tutor time at the end of the day, give every pupil a moment to reset, be heard and reconnect with an adult.

The afternoon is practical

Periods 4 and 5 are 60-minute blocks, so that practical, applied and offsite learning has the time it needs.

Escorted home

Pupils are escorted to their transport at 3.15pm, which keeps the end of the day calm and safe.

Our approach

Low threat, high challenge

Pupils can only access an ambitious curriculum once they feel safe, regulated and understood. We do not choose between care and challenge: we build both into every lesson.

Low threat

Calm, safe and predictable

Predictable routines and visual timetables. A small team of adults who know every pupil well. Time to regulate before learning, in our nurture and sensory regulation space or calm space. A fresh start after every difficult moment, with repair and reconnection rather than punishment.

High challenge

Ambitious for every pupil

The same ambitious content as pupils elsewhere wherever possible, with scaffolding and support rather than lowered expectations. High-quality programmes (White Rose Maths, White Rose Science and Pearson English), personal targets linked to each pupil's EHCP, and recognised qualifications from Year 7.

Low barrier

Removing what gets in the way

Pre-teaching of key vocabulary, visual supports and task boards, structured routines, clear learning objectives, access arrangements, and speech and language therapy or other clinical input where a pupil's EHCP requires it.

Children's rights

Working towards Rights Respecting Schools status

We are working towards Rights Respecting Schools status with UNICEF UK. This means putting children's rights at the heart of daily life, so that every pupil knows their rights and learns to respect the rights of others. Find out about Rights Respecting Schools.

Learning that counts

Reading, qualifications and the future

Our curriculum is ambitious, broad and carefully sequenced, and it prepares every pupil for the next stage of education, training or employment.

Reading every day

Every pupil reads for a protected 30 minutes each day, in addition to English lessons. We aim for every pupil to leave with a reading age beyond a functional level, and Reading Champions help to celebrate books and reading.

Qualifications from Year 7

Accreditation starts in Year 7 with the AQA Unit Award Scheme and Entry Level qualifications, and by Year 9 pupils can sit Level 1 Functional Skills. In Years 10 and 11 each pupil follows a personalised pathway that may include Functional Skills, Entry Level Certificates, GCSEs and vocational awards such as Basic First Aid and Food Hygiene.

Careers and the future

Every pupil has independent, impartial careers guidance. Pupils in Years 7 to 9 take part in a week of employer encounters, and pupils in Years 10 and 11 have taster days, work-related learning and work experience with vetted local employers.

Homework that helps

Homework is child led. Reading is the priority, and pupils choose whether, when and how to take learning home, with support from staff and families. No pupil is penalised for not doing it.

Pupil voice

Our School Council gives pupils a real voice in the school, and pupils' views shape curriculum planning and reviews.

Active and healthy

P.E., music and enrichment

We place a big emphasis on broad and inspiring opportunities through enrichment, so that young people can lead active, healthy lives.

Every week

Weekly P.E. lessons

We have a partnership with a local sports centre, which provides our P.E. facilities. Lessons are led by a qualified sports coach and follow an inspiring P.E. curriculum, giving every pupil the chance to experience success in a variety of sports.

  • Football
  • Swimming
  • Yoga and fitness
  • New sports, such as pickleball

Skills for life

Music

We see music as not just a fantastic creative outlet but a chance to develop skills and interests for life that can lead to future career opportunities. As such, we work with a local musician, spoken word artist and tutor, who brings individualised learning to our music sessions. Art, music and drama are also offered through our rolling programme of musician and artist in residence, so that every pupil experiences creative learning.

Beyond the classroom

Enrichment opportunities

Enrichment is part of the curriculum, not an extra. Pupils can try new challenges away from school and discover what they enjoy and what they are good at.

  • Sailing
  • Kayaking
  • Climbing

As confidence grows

The Duke of Edinburgh's Award

As young people's confidence grows, these opportunities will lead to accreditation through the Duke of Edinburgh's Award, recognising their commitment, skills and achievements.

Before you visit

Practical information for families

School meals

Breakfast is available on arrival at Arrival and Breakfast Club (8.45am). A hot lunch from a local provider is included, and dietary and allergy needs are catered for. Please tell the school office.

Uniform

White polo shirt or school shirt, black trousers, plain black sweatshirt, jumper or cardigan (not a hoody), and smart plain black footwear (no heels or flip flops). See full uniform details.

P.E. kit

Plain black P.E. shorts, jogging bottoms or tracksuit, a plain white t-shirt, and plimsolls or training shoes.

Equipment and phones

We provide all equipment, including stationery. Bags should hold only a pencil case, home/school diary, P.E. kit and sanitary products as needed. We are a mobile phone free school: any phone brought onto site is stored in a pouch at reception. Read our Mobile Phone Policy (BE-05).

Attendance

Many pupils have found attendance hard before. We work with pupils and families to understand and remove barriers, and share term dates and inset days well in advance.
